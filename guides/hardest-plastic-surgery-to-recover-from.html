What Is the Hardest Plastic Surgery to Recover From?

Seven procedures with demanding recoveries, what the first days are really like, and how to plan safe care after surgery

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · November 2026

If you are considering plastic surgery, you have probably compared before-and-after photos, researched surgeons, and looked at prices. But have you considered what happens during the first few days after the operation? How painful will it be? Will you be able to get out of bed? What happens if you become dizzy while walking to the bathroom or need help with surgical drains?

A lower body lift and a full tummy tuck with abdominal muscle repair are among the hardest cosmetic surgeries to recover from. A mommy makeover can be particularly demanding because several surgical areas heal together. A Brazilian butt lift (BBL) creates a different problem: restrictions on sitting and sleeping. There is no medically established universal ranking, because pain, mobility, the extent of surgery, underlying health, and complications all affect recovery.

Below are seven procedures patients should prepare for carefully, followed by an important answer to whether major plastic surgery patients should recover alone.

1. Lower Body Lift: Extensive Incisions and Limited Movement

A lower body lift is often performed after substantial weight loss. It removes loose skin around the abdomen, waist, hips, and buttocks. Unlike a standard tummy tuck, a circumferential body lift may involve an incision extending around much of the torso. Getting out of bed, using the bathroom, and finding a comfortable sleeping position can be difficult because movements create tension across healing tissues.

Patients may have drains to remove accumulated fluid. Swelling, discomfort, numbness, wound separation, seromas, infection, and delayed healing are potential concerns. Several weeks of activity restrictions are common, while swelling and sensation may continue changing for months. A recovery nurse can assist with transfers, vital signs, dressings, surgeon-directed drain care, and recognizing symptoms that need medical assessment.

Medical sources: ASPS body lift recovery and ASPS body lift safety.

2. Full Tummy Tuck With Muscle Repair: Difficult Everyday Movement

A tummy tuck, or abdominoplasty, removes excess abdominal skin and fat. When rectus diastasis is present, the surgeon may tighten the connective tissue between the abdominal muscles using sutures. The incision and abdominal wall tightening can make sitting up, standing, coughing, and changing positions uncomfortable. Some surgeons recommend remaining slightly bent at the waist initially to reduce tension on the incision.

A full tummy tuck may involve JP drains, prescribed compression garments, and restrictions on lifting or strenuous exercise. A patient may need help standing and taking the first short walks, even though gentle walking is usually encouraged to support circulation. Some patients return to desk work in a few weeks, while physical recovery and exercise restrictions can extend to six to eight weeks or longer. Your operating surgeon determines the timetable.

If you leave surgery with drains, drainage must be measured and recorded as instructed. An incorrectly fitted garment can also create pressure problems. Professional recovery care can assist with mobility, drain documentation, and monitoring for bleeding or changes in circulation.

Medical sources: ASPS tummy tuck recovery and ASPS tummy tuck recovery guidance.

3. Mommy Makeover: Multiple Surgical Sites Healing Together

A mommy makeover combines procedures such as a tummy tuck, breast lift, breast augmentation, or liposuction. The difficulty is easy to imagine: after abdominal muscle repair, getting out of bed is uncomfortable, while recent breast surgery may limit how comfortably you can use your arms. Caring for children, dressing yourself, and changing sleeping positions can require assistance.

A study published in the Aesthetic Surgery Journal in 2023 examined 28,171 patients who underwent selected cosmetic surgery procedures. Researchers reported that 1,400 patients (5.0%) experienced a complication within 30 days. The complication rate was 7.6% among patients undergoing combined procedures, compared with 4.2% among those undergoing single procedures. The findings varied depending on which surgical procedures were combined.

When several regions are treated at once, patients should arrange help for the initial recovery and for any longer activity limitations. Ask your surgeon how to manage sleeping, arm use, bending, lifting, and drains before the surgery date.

Research: Aesthetic Surgery Journal 2023 study and PubMed abstract.

4. Brazilian Butt Lift: Sitting and Sleeping Restrictions

A Brazilian butt lift removes fat by liposuction and transfers it to the buttocks. Patients must recover from both the liposuction areas and the grafted fat. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons advises avoiding prolonged sitting for about two weeks, with some surgeons recommending longer. Sleeping face down or on the side is commonly recommended, while specially positioned support may be required if sitting is unavoidable.

This can make eating, travelling, using the toilet, or getting comfortable at night difficult. Following the surgeon's instructions is essential: patients should not place direct pressure on grafted fat during the restricted period. The recovery also includes swelling, bruising, soreness, and compression garment instructions for liposuction areas.

A BBL has a specific serious risk of fat embolism during fat injection. Ask the surgeon about safe subcutaneous placement and real-time ultrasound guidance. Nursing after surgery cannot undo unsafe injection technique or eliminate the procedure's risks, but can help with positioning, observations, and recognizing symptoms needing urgent care.

Medical sources: ASPS buttock enhancement recovery and ASPS BBL safety information.

5. Extensive Liposuction and Lipo 360: Swelling, Drainage, and Fluid Changes

Liposuction may involve small skin incisions, but the area treated beneath the skin can be extensive. Lipo 360 usually addresses the abdomen, flanks, waist, and back. When several regions are treated, bending, twisting, and changing positions may hurt. Swelling, bruising, tenderness, and fluid drainage can also be substantial.

Large-volume procedures involve fluid administered during surgery, postoperative drainage, and potential blood loss. Changes in circulating volume can affect blood pressure and make standing difficult. Surgical teams assess the extent of liposuction and patient risk factors when determining monitoring and discharge needs.

A recovery nurse can observe blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation, pain, bleeding, and drainage; help with compression garments; and assist with walking when appropriate. Unexpected dizziness, fainting, weakness, or bleeding requires prompt assessment.

Medical source: ASPS recovery checklist.

6. Breast Augmentation Under the Muscle: Chest Tightness and Arm Restrictions

Implants placed beneath the pectoral muscle can cause significant early chest pressure or tightness. Getting dressed, reaching, using your arms to rise from bed, and lifting objects can be uncomfortable. Surgeons may prescribe a supportive bra and restrict upper-body exercise or lifting while tissues heal.

Acute pain often improves after the first several days, but soreness and swelling can continue for weeks. Recovery is more extensive when augmentation is combined with a breast lift or revision. Always follow the operating surgeon's specific movement and lifting restrictions.

Medical source: ASPS breast augmentation recovery.

7. Facelift and Neck Lift: Swelling and Important Bleeding Risks

Facelift patients sometimes report less pain than people recovering from extensive abdominal surgery, but swelling, bruising, tightness, and changes in sensation can still be uncomfortable. Sleeping with the head elevated and avoiding strenuous activity may be required. The NHS describes an initial recovery period of approximately two to four weeks, although visible and sensory changes can last longer.

A hematoma, or collection of blood beneath the skin, is an important potential complication after facial surgery. Rapidly increasing swelling around the neck can become an airway emergency. New breathing difficulty or rapidly expanding neck swelling requires emergency care rather than waiting for a routine follow-up.

Medical sources: ASPS facelift recovery and NHS facelift recovery.

Which Plastic Surgery Has the Longest Recovery?

It depends on whether you mean time off work, time needing help, or the months until swelling and tissue changes settle. These broad estimates describe recovery planning, not a ranking of risk.

What Is the Most Painful Plastic Surgery?

A tummy tuck with muscle repair may cause marked discomfort when you stand, cough, or move. A lower body lift affects a larger area and can create tension along several incisions. A mommy makeover combines surgical sites. Yet pain is personal: two people receiving the same operation can experience very different recoveries. Pain severity alone cannot tell you whether a complication is developing.

What Are the Hardest Days After Plastic Surgery?

For some patients, the first 24 to 72 hours are particularly difficult. As anesthesia wears off, pain, nausea, dizziness, swelling, and medication-related drowsiness may interfere with movement. A patient who feels well lying down may become lightheaded on standing. Surgical drains and dressings may also need attention. Not every patient follows the same timeline, and unexpectedly worsening symptoms require assessment.

Why is the first night after surgery important?

Bleeding, breathing difficulties, adverse medication effects, dizziness, and changes in circulation can develop early. After discharge, patients need appropriate supervision, help with mobility, and clear instructions on whom to contact. A Toronto hotel room is not a substitute for a safe postoperative care plan.

What Warning Signs Need Urgent Medical Attention?

Call 911 for sudden difficulty breathing, chest pain, collapse, or rapidly increasing neck swelling that may affect breathing. Seek prompt medical assessment for significant bleeding, rapidly expanding swelling, severe or unexpectedly increasing pain, new one-sided leg swelling or pain, or signs of infection such as fever with worsening wound redness or drainage. Do not wait for a routine clinic visit if symptoms may be serious.

Can You Recover From Major Plastic Surgery Alone?

No. Recovering alone after major plastic surgery is unsafe and could put your health in serious jeopardy. Major plastic surgery is not something you should plan to recover from without assistance. Whether you are having a tummy tuck, Brazilian butt lift (BBL), Lipo 360, mommy makeover, or lower body lift, you will need someone to help you during the initial postoperative recovery period.

After surgery, you may experience pain, dizziness, nausea, weakness, or difficulty moving independently. The effects of anesthesia and prescribed pain medications can also affect coordination, balance, and judgment. Even getting out of bed or walking to the bathroom can become dangerous if you feel faint.

Patients recovering from a tummy tuck with muscle repair need assistance getting out of bed and changing positions without placing unnecessary tension on their abdominal incisions. After a BBL, patients need help following positioning instructions to avoid pressure on transferred fat. Extensive liposuction may involve substantial drainage, swelling, blood loss, and changes in body fluid distribution that affect blood pressure and circulation.

Some patients are discharged with JP or Hemovac drains that require monitoring, measuring, and recording drainage. Dressings and compression garments also need to be managed according to surgeon instructions.

The first 24 hours after major plastic surgery are particularly important. Bleeding, respiratory complications, abnormal blood pressure, and adverse medication effects can develop during this period. Complications can become life-threatening without prompt attention.

A responsible adult must be part of the discharge plan where required after general anesthesia or sedation, and patients undergoing extensive procedures should arrange assistance beyond the first 24 hours according to their surgeon's instructions. Family can help with daily activities, but family members may not recognize early complications. A Registered Nurse can provide clinical monitoring, recovery assistance, and timely escalation when medical attention is necessary.

If you are planning major plastic surgery in Toronto, do not arrange to recover alone. Have suitable help organized before the operation. Toronto ProRN provides private recovery nursing in homes, hotels, and suitable accommodations across Toronto and the GTA.

Should You Hire a Private Recovery Nurse After Plastic Surgery?

For major operations, professional recovery nursing can provide clinical monitoring alongside routine assistance. Family members can prepare meals and help with daily tasks, but usually are not trained to assess postoperative bleeding, breathing changes, abnormal vital signs, or problems involving drains. An ICU-trained recovery nurse can identify concerning changes, deliver nursing care within professional scope, and contact the surgeon or emergency services when needed. Nursing cannot replace your surgical team or remove all risks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a tummy tuck more painful than a C-section?

Some people who have experienced both report greater discomfort from a tummy tuck, while others report the opposite. Surgical techniques and individual recovery circumstances differ, so there is no reliable universal answer.

Is BBL recovery harder than tummy tuck recovery?

A tummy tuck with muscle repair can make standing, coughing, and rising from bed painful. A BBL adds restrictions on sitting and sleeping, as well as discomfort from liposuction. The harder experience depends on the patient and procedure.

Why can't you stand upright after a tummy tuck?

Some surgeons recommend a slightly bent position early after surgery to reduce tension on the incision. The surgeon will advise when you can gradually stand more upright.

Which plastic surgery requires the most bed rest?

Extended bed rest is generally not the goal. Gentle walking is often encouraged to maintain circulation, with timing and restrictions determined by the surgeon.

Is the third day after surgery always the most painful?

No. Some people feel more swelling or discomfort after the first day, but there is no single worst day for all patients. Unexpectedly severe or increasing pain should be assessed.

How long should someone stay with you after surgery?

Follow the surgeon's discharge directions. Supervision is typically required following general anesthesia or sedation, and patients having major procedures should arrange the assistance they need for longer than the first day when instructed.

Can a private nurse stay overnight after plastic surgery in Toronto?

Yes. Toronto ProRN provides advance-booked overnight nursing subject to availability. An ICU-trained Registered Nurse can monitor and document care and assist with postoperative needs.

Choosing a surgeon and planning your procedure are important decisions. So is deciding who will assist you when you leave the operating facility. Consider mobility, drains, medications, your sleeping position, and who will notice a concerning change.
