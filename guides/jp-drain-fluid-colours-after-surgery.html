Surgical Drains

What Do Different JP Drain Fluid Colours Mean After Surgery?

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · March 2026

You’ve just had a tummy tuck, breast operation, or another procedure, and you’ve been discharged with a surgical drain. Yesterday the fluid looked dark red. Today it looks pink, and you may be wondering whether that’s normal.

JP drain fluid commonly changes in colour as healing progresses, but a change in colour alone can’t tell you whether everything is fine. The amount, smell, consistency, drain function, and how you feel also matter.

What Is a JP Drain?

A Jackson-Pratt (JP) drain is a closed suction device. A flexible tube removes blood and other fluid from a surgical area into a squeezable collection bulb. Not every plastic surgery patient needs one. Your surgeon decides whether drains are indicated and when they should be removed.

The US National Library of Medicine’s MedlinePlus instructions explain how these drains work and describe care at home.

What Does Dark Red JP Drainage Mean?

Dark red or bloody drainage may occur early after surgery. Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center notes that drainage can begin dark red and then lighten over time. However, a sudden increase in bright-red fluid or blood, particularly with dizziness, increasing swelling, or weakness, needs immediate contact with your surgical team or urgent assessment.

Don’t judge bleeding by colour alone. A dramatic increase in the number of millilitres collected can be as important as what the fluid looks like.

What Does Pink JP Drainage Mean?

Pink or reddish-pink drainage is often called serosanguineous fluid. It contains both blood and serous fluid produced during tissue healing. It can be part of an expected progression from darker red drainage to lighter fluid. The trend should still be evaluated against your surgeon’s instructions and the amount collected.

What Does Clear or Pale Yellow JP Drainage Mean?

Clear or straw-coloured drainage may be serous fluid. This appearance can occur as the amount of blood in the fluid decreases. However, don’t assume that everything yellow is normal. A thick, cloudy, foul-smelling or pus-like discharge needs assessment, especially with fever, increased redness, or pain.

What Does Cloudy, Green or Foul-Smelling Drainage Mean?

Cloudiness, pus-like consistency, a foul smell, or new greenish drainage can be associated with infection or another complication. Colour alone does not diagnose an infection, but these changes warrant prompt contact with the surgeon. Watch for redness spreading around the insertion site, warmth, fever, and increasing tenderness.

What if Your JP Drain Suddenly Stops Draining?

A sudden decrease can mean less fluid is being produced, but it can also mean the tube is kinked, obstructed, displaced, or the bulb has lost suction. Check only what your care instructions tell you to check. Do not pull, remove, flush or manipulate the tube beyond techniques your surgical team has specifically taught you. Contact the surgeon if the drain stops unexpectedly or you notice swelling while output falls.

How Much JP Drainage Is Normal?

There is no single normal daily amount for every operation. Output depends on the location and extent of surgery and where you are in recovery. Your surgeon may use a specific millilitre-per-day threshold when deciding when a drain can be removed.

Measure and document each drain separately. Record the time, amount in millilitres, and appearance. If you have two drains, don’t combine their results unless specifically instructed to do so. MSK’s patient instructions include practical drain recording advice.

When Should You Call Your Surgeon?

Call your surgical team promptly if you notice a sudden increase in bloody output, thick or foul-smelling drainage, increasing redness, fever, worsening pain, drainage that stops unexpectedly, or a bulb that won’t stay compressed. Your postoperative team may give you additional thresholds specific to your procedure.

Call 911 for severe bleeding, difficulty breathing, chest pain, or collapse. Don’t delay emergency care while waiting for a routine callback.

Who Helps With JP Drains After Surgery?

Before discharge, your surgical team should teach you and your designated helper to empty the bulb, restore suction, measure output and keep the insertion site clean according to their protocol. After a major tummy tuck or breast procedure, you may not be able to bend or reach the tubing comfortably. Arrange help before surgery rather than trying to manage it alone while dizzy or in pain.

Can you manage your drains and recover alone after major plastic surgery? No. Don’t plan to be alone during the initial postoperative period. You’ll need someone with you to help.
