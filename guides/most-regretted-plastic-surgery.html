What Is the Most Regretted Plastic Surgery? A Post-Op Nurse Reviews the Research

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · November 2026

Breast implants, nose jobs, body contouring, buccal fat removal and fillers top the regret lists. Here's what the published numbers say and how Toronto patients can plan surgery they'll still like in ten years.

Which Plastic Surgery Do Patients Regret the Most?

Search results on this topic usually name breast augmentation and rhinoplasty first, followed by Brazilian butt lifts, buccal fat removal and filler. The published research gives a more detailed answer. A 2024 systematic review in The American Journal of Surgery included a total of 55 articles examining regret after plastic surgery. It found regret rates of 5.1% to 9.1% in breast augmentation and 10.82% to 33.3% in body contouring. So by percentage, body contouring procedures like tummy tucks, body lifts and liposuction had the highest regret range in that review. Breast implants rank high for another reason, which is the number of patients who need another operation later. Rhinoplasty ranks high because the nose sits in the centre of the face and small changes are easy to notice.

Why Do Breast Implants Rank So High for Regret and Revision?

The number you see online, that up to 40% of women regret their implants, comes from FDA reoperation data and not from regret surveys. The FDA reported that 20% to 40% of women receiving implants for augmentation had a reoperation in the first eight to ten years. A large FDA cohort of almost 100,000 patients found that at seven years, the reoperation rate was 11.7% for primary augmentation, and capsular contracture was the most common reason for reoperation among augmentations. Capsular contracture means the scar tissue around the implant hardens and squeezes it, which can change the breast's shape and cause pain. Patients also pick sizes in their twenties that may not suit their bodies after pregnancy, weight changes and aging. In the United States, data from both major plastic surgery groups shows twice as many explantations performed today as in 2015, while augmentation numbers have stayed about level. In 2015, people under 30 accounted for a third of breast enlargements, according to ASPS. But today, they account for only 17% of those surgeries.

Some women also have their silicone implants removed because of symptoms they link to breast implant illness (BII), such as joint pain, fatigue and memory problems. The Food and Drug Administration notes that BII is not recognized as a formal diagnosis and there are no tests or clinical criteria to define it. Women with these symptoms should still report them to their surgeon and family doctor. The FDA has also warned that "the longer you have your implants, the more likely it will be for you to have them removed." A patient planning augmentation should budget for at least one future surgery. Recovery after explant surgery needs planning too, especially when a breast lift is done at the same time.

Why Is Rhinoplasty Revised So Frequently?

Research puts the surgical revision rate for rhinoplasty at 5% to 15%. One review of 369 cosmetic rhinoplasties at a group practice found overall complication, dissatisfaction and revision rates were 7.9%, 15.4%, and 9.8%, respectively. Patients who already had a revision are more likely to need another, with one report finding 10.5% for primary rhinoplasty and 23.9% for revision surgery. The nose heals slowly, and swelling in the tip can last a year or longer. In a survey of rhinoplasty surgeons, most surgeons thought that at least one year should elapse after the first operation before revision. Patients who judge their result at three months may end up unhappy with a nose that hasn't finished healing. Revision surgery is also harder because cartilage has been removed and scar tissue has formed.

Do People Regret Getting a Brazilian Butt Lift?

BBLs fall under body contouring, the category with the highest regret range in the 2024 review. Some of the regret comes from size. The very large, rounded shape popular on social media a few years ago is requested less now, and some patients go back for surgery to reduce it. Other regret comes from recovery, which involves weeks of not sitting directly on the buttocks and sleeping on the stomach or side. Safety is also a concern with this surgery. Fat injected into or under the gluteal muscle can enter large veins and travel to the lungs, which can be fatal. Surgeons now inject fat only into the layer above the muscle, and patients should ask their surgeon to confirm this before booking.

Why Are Surgeons Seeing Buccal Fat Removal Regret?

Buccal fat removal takes out part of a fat pad in the lower cheek to give the face a more sculpted look. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) didn't track buccal fat removal before 2019. By 2022, over 4,500 procedures had been documented among ASPS-member surgeons alone. The procedure is permanent, and if too much buccal fat is removed early on, the person may later feel they have lost volume too quickly. Faces lose volume naturally with age, so cheeks that look sculpted at 25 can look hollow at 45. Surgeons can try to restore the area later with filler or fat grafting, but some plastic surgeons are starting to see a bit of blowback from the buccal removal craze. Some patients end up with prematurely wizened cheeks that are hard to restore to their original proportions. Patients with a long or narrow face and flat cheekbones are usually poor candidates. A surgeon who says no to this procedure is giving useful advice.

Can Lip and Under-Eye Filler Be Reversed?

Patients regret hyaluronic acid filler too. ASPS recorded 1,449,565 lip augmentation procedures with injectable materials in 2024. Overfilled lips can look swollen, and filler can move outside the lip border over time. Under-eye filler can hold fluid and make the area look puffy instead of smooth. Hyaluronic acid filler can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase, which a qualified injector should keep on hand. Dissolving filler can take more than one appointment and can cause swelling for a few days. Patients should ask about the injector's training and how they handle complications before treatment.

What Causes Regret After Cosmetic Surgery?

A 2023 review in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open found that preoperative education, decisional tools and postoperative complications were the most important factors affecting decision regret after surgery. In breast reconstruction research, having a postoperative complication led to significantly greater levels of decision regret without impacting satisfaction with breasts. Body dysmorphic disorder is another cause. A 2024 meta-analysis of 65 papers found the overall estimated prevalence of BDD was 18.6% among plastic surgery patients. In one facial surgery clinic, those seeking revision rhinoplasty had higher rates of positive BDD screens. People with BDD usually stay unhappy after surgery because the problem is in how they see themselves. Trends play a part too, since a look that's popular at 25 may not suit a 45-year-old face.

How Can Patients Lower Their Risk of Regret?

Patients should choose a plastic surgeon certified by the Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada and ask to see results on people with a similar body type. They should ask how the result will look at six months, ten years and twenty years. For implants, they should ask about the expected lifespan and the cost of future surgery. Waiting a few months between the consultation and the surgery date gives time to think without a sales deadline. If a surgeon or a friend says the patient may not be a good candidate, that opinion deserves serious thought. Patients with a history of depression, anxiety or intense focus on one feature should talk with their doctor first.

How Recovery Care Affects How Patients Feel About Their Results

Since complications can raise the amount of regret, the recovery period has a direct effect on how patients feel about their surgery. Infections, seromas, hematomas and wound openings are easier to treat when someone notices them early. In the first days after surgery, patients are sore, on pain medication and in no condition to judge their own drain output, swelling or breathing. A trained nurse checks vital signs, incisions, drains and compression garments, and calls the surgeon when something looks wrong. Patients who have a nurse with them are also more likely to follow restrictions on lifting, sitting and walking. Good recovery care can't fix a poor surgical choice, but it lowers the chance of a complication that leads to regret.

Can you recover alone after major plastic surgery? The answer is no, so don't plan to be alone. You'll need someone with you during the first days after surgery. If family or friends can't stay, you can book a private recovery nurse after plastic surgery in Toronto.
