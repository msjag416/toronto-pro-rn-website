What Nobody Explains About Recovering From Plastic Surgery: The First 72 Hours

Why planning your recovery is as important as choosing your plastic surgeon

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · July 2026

You've researched your plastic surgeon, examined before-and-after photographs, read patient reviews, and scheduled your procedure. You've probably spent considerable time deciding what results you want and preparing for your surgery.

But how much thought have you given to what happens after you're discharged?

Whether you're having a tummy tuck, liposuction 360, Brazilian butt lift (BBL), breast augmentation, breast reduction, or a combination of procedures, the first few days of recovery can be considerably more demanding than you expect.

Patients often prepare for the operation itself without fully understanding how much physical assistance and medical supervision they may need afterward.

Your surgery may take only a few hours, but your recovery continues long after you've left the operating room.

The First 24 Hours: You're Home, but You're Still Recovering From Anesthesia

Being discharged from a surgical facility means your medical team has determined that you meet the criteria for discharge. It doesn't mean the effects of anesthesia have completely disappeared or that you're ready to manage everything independently.

Depending on your operation, the medications administered, and your medical history, you may experience drowsiness, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, weakness, and postoperative pain.

Standing or walking for the first time can be particularly difficult. Some patients experience a sudden drop in blood pressure when changing positions, causing lightheadedness or fainting.

You may also have surgical dressings, compression garments, or drains requiring attention.

Following extensive liposuction, blood-tinged drainage from incision sites can be expected, although excessive bleeding or changes in drainage require assessment.

You should have a responsible adult accompany you home and remain with you for the period specified by your surgical team, commonly at least 24 hours following anesthesia.

This is particularly important if you're taking prescribed opioid pain medication or other medications that cause sedation.

Why professional nursing care can make a difference

A postoperative registered nurse can assess your blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, pain level, and overall recovery while following your surgeon's postoperative instructions.

At Toronto ProRN Inc., our nursing services may include assistance with prescribed medications, nausea management, drainage monitoring, positioning, and safe mobilization.

We also assess for early signs of postoperative complications and communicate concerns to your surgeon when necessary.

An Uber driver provides transportation. An Uber driver isn't your postoperative caregiver.

If you're travelling to Toronto for plastic surgery, arranging transportation home is only one part of your recovery plan.

Hours 24-48: Everyday Activities Can Become Surprisingly Difficult

Think about everything you normally do without assistance.

Getting out of bed, using the bathroom, preparing something to eat, taking prescribed medication, or changing your clothing may require help after surgery.

Following a tummy tuck with abdominal muscle repair, even moving from a lying position to standing can be uncomfortable.

Patients recovering from liposuction 360 may experience significant swelling, bruising, tenderness, and fluid drainage from several areas of the body.

After a Brazilian butt lift, your surgeon may restrict sitting or lying directly on your buttocks to protect newly transferred fat cells. These restrictions affect how you sleep, rest, travel, and perform everyday activities.

Following breast augmentation, breast reduction, or breast lift surgery, reaching overhead and using your arms to push yourself out of bed may be uncomfortable or restricted.

Your surgeon's specific postoperative instructions determine which activities are permitted.

Who's going to help you?

At Toronto ProRN Inc., our private nursing services are designed to assist patients with the physical demands of recovery.

Depending on your surgical procedure and prescribed care plan, your registered nurse can assist with:

  • Safe mobilization and positioning: Helping you get out of bed, walk to the bathroom, and change positions while following surgical restrictions.
  • Vital-sign monitoring: Assessing blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, temperature, and other relevant clinical findings.
  • Surgical drain management: Emptying, measuring, and documenting output from Jackson-Pratt drains or Hemovac drains when ordered.
  • Compression garments: Helping adjust or manage prescribed postoperative garments while protecting incisions and surgical areas.
  • Medication management: Administering prescribed medications within the authorized nursing care plan and monitoring for adverse effects.
  • Hydration and nutrition: Encouraging appropriate fluid intake and assisting with meals according to your postoperative instructions.
  • Monitoring for complications: Recognizing concerning changes that require communication with your surgeon or emergency medical assessment.

Having an experienced registered nurse present can be particularly beneficial when you're recovering from extensive or combined surgical procedures.

Hours 48-72: Recovery Isn't Always Easier on the Second or Third Day

You may expect to feel considerably better after your first night at home, but postoperative recovery doesn't always follow that pattern.

Swelling and bruising may become more noticeable during the second and third days. Pain may also become more apparent as long-acting local anesthetics wear off.

If you've undergone extensive liposuction, you may continue experiencing swelling, tenderness, and drainage.

You might also be dealing with constipation caused by opioid pain medication, reduced mobility, interrupted sleep, and a decreased appetite.

Some patients become concerned because they don't know whether their symptoms are expected or indicate a complication.

Managing drains, compression garments, and postoperative medications

Patients discharged with surgical drains need clear instructions about emptying the collection reservoirs, measuring output, and recognizing changes that require medical attention.

Jackson-Pratt and Hemovac drains may be used following certain tummy tuck, breast surgery, or other surgical procedures.

Your registered nurse can document the amount and appearance of drainage and maintain a record for your surgeon.

Compression garments also require attention. Following a tummy tuck or liposuction 360, garments must provide the prescribed compression without creating excessive pressure, restricting breathing, or interfering with wound healing.

For BBL patients, garment positioning is particularly important because excessive pressure over newly grafted areas may affect recovery.

Some patients are discharged with a peripheral intravenous catheter or saline lock for prescribed follow-up treatment. These devices require an appropriate clinical plan, assessment, maintenance, and care by qualified healthcare professionals.

Your postoperative care should follow your surgeon's written orders, including medication instructions, drain management, compression requirements, and activity restrictions.

What About Complications After Plastic Surgery?

Most patients recover without major complications, but cosmetic surgery can involve serious postoperative risks.

Extensive liposuction, particularly when several areas are treated, may involve blood loss and changes in fluid distribution that affect circulating blood volume and blood pressure.

Postoperative complications can include hematoma, seroma, infection, wound separation, and venous thromboembolism.

A pulmonary embolism is a medical emergency requiring immediate treatment.

Warning signs that should never be ignored

Seek emergency medical attention if you develop sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, new confusion, or significant uncontrolled bleeding.

Rapidly worsening swelling, severe or increasing pain, fever, concerning wound changes, or unusual drainage require prompt assessment by your surgical team.

Not every postoperative complication begins with dramatic symptoms.

Changes in vital signs, worsening weakness, unexpected drainage, or other concerning clinical findings may indicate a developing problem.

An ICU-trained registered nurse has experience assessing patients, recognizing changes in their condition, and responding appropriately when additional medical attention is required.

However, private nursing care doesn't replace your surgeon's follow-up appointments, emergency medical services, or hospital treatment when indicated.

Recovering in a Toronto Hotel or Airbnb After Plastic Surgery?

Toronto attracts patients travelling from other Canadian provinces and international destinations for cosmetic surgery.

If you're visiting Toronto for a tummy tuck, liposuction 360, Brazilian butt lift, breast surgery, or a combination of procedures, you'll need to arrange appropriate care before arriving.

Recovering in a hotel or Airbnb can present difficulties, especially if you're travelling alone.

Who will accompany you back to your accommodation after surgery?

Who will remain with you during the required postoperative supervision period?

Who will help you get out of bed, use the bathroom, manage your drains, and take your prescribed medications?

How will you attend follow-up appointments if you're unable to drive or travel comfortably?

And what will happen if you develop concerning symptoms overnight?

A hotel room may provide comfortable accommodation, but it doesn't provide nursing care.

Can you recover alone in a hotel or Airbnb after major plastic surgery? No. Wherever you're staying, don't plan to spend your first days after surgery by yourself. You'll need assistance during the initial postoperative period, and someone should be with you.

Do You Need a Registered Nurse After Plastic Surgery?

Not every patient requires private nursing care.

Your need for professional postoperative assistance depends on the type and extent of your operation, medical history, discharge instructions, and whether you have a capable adult available to care for you.

Patients recovering from extensive liposuction, abdominoplasty with muscle repair, BBL, or combined cosmetic procedures may require additional assistance with mobility, drains, compression garments, and postoperative monitoring.

If you're travelling alone or staying in unfamiliar accommodation, arranging professional nursing support may also be worth considering.

Before Booking Plastic Surgery, Plan Your Recovery

You've invested time researching your procedure and selecting your surgeon. Your postoperative arrangements deserve the same attention.

Before surgery, ask your surgeon how long you'll need someone to stay with you, what assistance you'll require, and whether your procedure or medical history warrants professional nursing care.

Discuss any surgical drains, compression garments, prescribed medications, mobility restrictions, and follow-up appointments.

If you're travelling to Toronto for surgery, make these arrangements before your arrival.

Don't wait until you're discharged to discover that you need more help than you anticipated.
