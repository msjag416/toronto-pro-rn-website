Compression Garments

How Tight Should a Compression Garment Be After a Tummy Tuck?

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · February 2026

You’ve had a tummy tuck, and now you’re wearing the abdominal binder or compression garment your surgeon prescribed. But how tight should it feel? Is a little indentation normal? What if it feels tighter as swelling increases?

Your garment should provide the support prescribed by your surgeon without severe pain, impaired breathing, persistent numbness, or skin injury. A comfortable level of compression depends on the procedure and garment design. There isn’t one safe tightness setting for everyone.

Why Do Surgeons Recommend Compression Garments After a Tummy Tuck?

Postoperative binders and garments can support the abdomen and may help manage swelling during recovery. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons’ tummy tuck guidance describes the use of an elastic bandage or compression garment after surgery. Your surgeon will decide whether you need one, which design to use, and when and how to wear it.

Garments don’t prevent every complication and shouldn’t replace monitoring of your wounds or symptoms.

How Tight Is Too Tight?

A garment is too tight if it causes significant pain, trouble breathing, persistent tingling or numbness, unusual skin discolouration, or pressure injury. A folded seam or tight band can concentrate pressure on one small area, especially near an incision, drain, or sensitive patch of skin.

Check the fit according to your surgeon’s instructions, and report concerning symptoms promptly. If you have chest pain or breathing difficulty, seek emergency medical care rather than assuming the garment is the only cause.

Is It Normal for a Compression Garment to Leave Marks?

Some temporary indentations from fabric or seams can occur. Deep or persistent marks, blisters, broken skin, discolouration, or pain are not signs to ignore. Reduced sensation after surgery means you may not notice pressure injury immediately. Assess the skin when you’re permitted to remove or adjust the garment.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons’ discussion of compression garments and incision care warns about problems associated with moisture, friction and improperly managed garments.

Can a Garment Put Too Much Pressure on Your Incision?

Yes. Rolling fabric, poorly positioned seams, or excessive compression can irritate healing incisions and drain sites. The right amount of compression is not simply ‘as tight as possible.’ Don’t place extra padding, tighten straps, or change to a smaller size without instructions from the surgical team.

What if Your Garment Gets Tighter as Swelling Increases?

Swelling can change during early recovery, so a garment that fit earlier may become uncomfortable. Increasing tightness should prompt you to check for skin changes and contact your surgeon if symptoms are concerning. Sudden or rapidly increasing abdominal swelling, especially with worsening pain or dizziness, requires medical assessment and should not automatically be blamed on normal healing.

Should You Buy a Smaller Garment When Swelling Improves?

Not unless your surgeon recommends it. A different stage of recovery may require a different fit, but smaller isn’t necessarily better. Ask the clinic whether your garment needs refitting and how to protect the incision and drain sites.

Can You Sleep in Your Compression Garment?

Some surgeons recommend wearing a prescribed garment overnight during the early recovery period. Others use different schedules. Follow your own surgeon’s instructions about overnight wear, bathing, removal and replacement. Don’t tolerate significant pain, numbness or skin injury just to keep the garment on.

Who Can Help You Manage It After Surgery?

A full tummy tuck, especially with muscle repair, can make bending and changing clothes difficult. You may also have surgical drains that must not be tugged or trapped under fabric. You will need appropriate postoperative assistance, especially during the initial period after anesthesia.

Can you recover alone after a tummy tuck? No. Don’t plan to be alone during the initial postoperative period. You’ll need someone with you to help.

An experienced postoperative Registered Nurse can help with the prescribed garment and assess skin condition, incision sites, drain tubing, swelling, and symptoms within the nursing care plan.
