Weight Loss · Loose Skin Surgery

Lost weight on Ozempic or Wegovy and left with loose skin? When did your surgeon say you were ready?

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · August 2026

Losing 40, 60, or even 100 pounds with Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound can have a positive impact on your health and appearance. But what happens when you’ve reached your goal weight and you’re left with loose, hanging skin around your stomach, arms, thighs, breasts, or buttocks?

For some people, loose skin becomes another reason to be frustrated after months of successful weight loss. Clothes don’t fit right, skin folds can become irritated, and their bodies can look different from what they might have expected. Some people also notice that their buttocks have become smaller or flatter because they’ve lost fat in that area. This is commonly called “Ozempic butt,” although it can happen after any significant weight loss, no matter how the weight was lost.

Plastic surgeons are seeing increased interest in surgery to remove excess skin after major weight loss.

According to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), 82% of its member surgeons reported receiving consultation requests related to GLP-1 weight-loss medications during 2025. Upper body lifts increased by 22%, arm lifts by 21%, and thigh lifts by 20% compared with the year before. These increases represent overall surgical activity, including patients who didn’t use GLP-1 medications.

RealSelf also reported a 31% increase in people viewing body lift information during the first quarter of 2026.

How long should your weight be stable before loose skin removal surgery?

This is one of the most important questions to ask before scheduling a tummy tuck, arm lift, thigh lift, or body lift.

A lot of plastic surgeons recommend keeping your weight stable for at least 3 to 6 months before surgery. Some recommend waiting six months or longer, depending on how much weight you’ve lost, whether you’re planning to lose more, and your overall health.

Why is waiting important?

If you continue losing a significant amount of weight after surgery, you could develop more loose skin. This could end up affecting your results and leave you considering another operation.

Your surgeon will also want to know whether you’re eating enough protein, getting the nutrients your body needs, and maintaining a weight you can realistically keep long-term.

Losing weight quickly can sometimes lead to nutritional deficiencies, especially if your appetite has decreased significantly while taking GLP-1 medications. Poor nutrition can affect how well your surgical wounds heal.

Can you still take Ozempic or Wegovy before plastic surgery?

Yes, some patients can continue taking GLP-1 medications before surgery, but the decision depends on their individual circumstances.

Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound slow down how quickly food leaves your stomach. This is important during anesthesia because stomach contents can enter your lungs if you vomit or regurgitate while sedated.

Your surgeon and anesthesia provider need to know which medication you’re taking, your dose, whether you’ve recently increased it, and whether you’ve experienced nausea, vomiting, bloating, or other stomach problems.

Some patients may need changes to their medication schedule or dietary instructions before surgery.

Don’t stop taking your prescribed medication without discussing it with your surgical and prescribing teams.

What plastic surgery procedures can remove loose skin after weight loss?

The procedure you need depends on where the loose skin is located and how much excess skin you have.

  • Tummy tuck (abdominoplasty): Removes excess skin and some fat from the abdomen. It can also repair separated abdominal muscles when necessary.
  • Panniculectomy: Removes the hanging skin and fat from the lower abdomen. This may be considered when excess skin causes repeated rashes, irritation, or difficulty with personal hygiene.
  • Arm lift (brachioplasty): Removes loose skin from the upper arms, particularly between the armpit and elbow.
  • Thigh lift: Removes excess skin from the inner or outer thighs, depending on the procedure.
  • Lower body lift: Removes loose skin around the abdomen, hips, and buttocks.
  • Breast lift (mastopexy): Raises and reshapes breasts that have become saggy after weight loss.

Some patients need more than one procedure because loose skin affects so many different areas of their bodies. Surgeons may recommend separating operations to reduce surgical risks and allow enough time for recovery, or they may perform a few procedures during the same appointment.

Every procedure involves surgical incisions and permanent scars. Recovery time depends on the type of surgery, the amount of skin removed, and your general health.

What is recovery like after loose skin removal surgery?

People considering a tummy tuck or body lift sometimes underestimate how much help they’ll need during the first few days after surgery.

You’ll most likely need assistance getting out of bed, walking to the bathroom, washing up, adjusting or changing compression garments, and managing surgical drains.

So can you recover on your own after a tummy tuck, body lift or other skin removal surgery? No. These are major operations, and you shouldn’t be alone during the initial recovery period. Arrange for a responsible adult or a registered nurse to stay with you before your surgery date, not after you’re discharged. You can book a private nurse for your body lift or tummy tuck recovery in Toronto.

Some patients go home with Jackson-Pratt drains, which collect blood and fluid from the surgical area. The drainage needs to be measured and recorded according to the surgeon’s instructions.

Your surgeon will ask you to wear a compression garment to help control swelling and support the surgical area. These garments should only be changed or removed according to the surgeon’s orders.

Possible complications include bleeding, infection, fluid collecting beneath the skin (seroma), wounds opening, and blood clots in the legs or lungs.

Significant bleeding can cause a drop in blood pressure and an increase in heart rate. A blood clot that travels to the lungs can cause sudden shortness of breath, chest pain, or fainting and requires emergency medical attention.

If you’re travelling to Toronto for plastic surgery, make sure you’ve arranged appropriate postoperative care before your operation.

You should know who will help you during recovery, how you’ll attend follow-up appointments, and how and when to contact your surgeon if you develop a problem.
