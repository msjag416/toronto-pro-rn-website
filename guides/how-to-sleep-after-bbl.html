How Do You Sleep After a Brazilian Butt Lift (BBL)?

JoAnne Gomez, RN

27+ years ICU · 14+ years plastic surgery recovery · April 2026

You’ve had your Brazilian butt lift, you’re back at your hotel or home, and now you’re wondering how you’re supposed to sleep comfortably without putting pressure on your buttocks. If you normally sleep on your back, this requires some changes.

After a Brazilian butt lift (BBL), surgeons generally instruct patients to avoid sleeping directly on their buttocks during the initial recovery period. The operation involves transferring fat into the buttocks, and protecting the grafted area from prolonged pressure is part of postoperative care. So what’s the best position, and for how long?

Can You Sleep on Your Back After a BBL?

Generally, no. During the initial recovery period, avoid sleeping directly on your back if doing so places pressure on the fat grafted into your buttocks. Your operating surgeon should tell you exactly how to position yourself.

During a BBL, fat is removed by liposuction, processed, and transferred to the buttocks. The transferred fat must establish a blood supply in its new location. Pressure on the grafted tissue is one reason surgeons recommend careful positioning.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons’ recovery guidance describes sleeping face down or on the side and avoiding prolonged sitting for approximately two weeks, although restrictions may last longer depending on the surgeon’s instructions.

Is It Better to Sleep on Your Stomach or Your Side?

Sleeping on your stomach helps avoid pressure directly on the buttocks. However, your abdomen may be uncomfortable if extensive liposuction was performed there. Side sleeping may feel better for some patients, but it can also place pressure on the outer hip if fat was transferred to that area.

Don’t assume that side sleeping is safe for everyone. Ask your surgeon whether fat was grafted to the lateral hips and which positions are appropriate in your specific case. A nurse can help you change positions while following those instructions.

How Long Do You Have to Sleep on Your Stomach After a BBL?

There isn’t one recovery schedule for every patient. Some surgeons restrict direct pressure for around two weeks, while others prescribe longer periods. The amount and location of fat transfer, concurrent liposuction, and your surgeon’s protocol all affect the instructions.

Don’t resume back sleeping just because your pain or swelling has improved. Ask your surgeon when sleeping positions can be changed.

What if You Accidentally Roll Onto Your Back While Sleeping?

If you wake up on your back, return to the prescribed position. A brief accidental change doesn’t automatically mean the fat transfer has failed. If this happens repeatedly or you can’t maintain the recommended position, call your surgical team for advice rather than improvising a restraint or special device.

Why Can Sleeping After a BBL Be So Uncomfortable?

You’re recovering from both liposuction and fat grafting. The abdomen, back, flanks, or waist may be swollen and tender, while the newly grafted buttocks require protection from direct pressure. Turning over, getting out of bed, and using the bathroom may require help.

Some patients have drainage from liposuction incisions. Your surgeon may prescribe a compression garment for treated liposuction areas. The garment should be fitted as directed, without inappropriate pressure on the grafted areas.

Can You Recover Alone After a BBL?

No. Don’t plan to recover alone immediately after major BBL surgery. You will need appropriate assistance in the initial postoperative period, particularly after general anesthesia, sedation, or extensive liposuction. Dizziness, nausea, weakness, and limited mobility can make an unassisted trip to the bathroom unsafe.

A responsible adult should be present according to the discharge plan. You may need help beyond the first night depending on the extent of surgery and your surgeon’s instructions. Anyone travelling to Toronto for a BBL should arrange recovery assistance before booking their hotel.

When Should You Seek Medical Help?

Contact your surgeon promptly for unexpected bleeding, rapidly worsening swelling, increasing redness, fever, or pain that is suddenly becoming worse. Call 911 for difficulty breathing, chest pain, fainting with concerning symptoms, or another medical emergency. BBL carries specific risks, including fat embolism, and nursing observation does not remove those risks.

For procedural safety information, see ASPS: Buttock Enhancement Risks and Safety.
