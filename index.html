Hour by hour

What your first night with a recovery nurse looks like

This is the timeline your nurse follows from the moment you arrive at your recovery address until your surgeon's office opens the next morning.

  1. HOUR 0 · ARRIVAL

    Your nurse meets you at your recovery address

    Your nurse is at your home, hotel or recovery suite when you get there, goes through your surgeon's written post-op orders with you, confirms your medication list and drain count, and gets you settled into bed safely. No handoff gets lost.

  2. HOURS 1–4 · HIGHEST RISK

    Vitals every 30 to 60 minutes

    Blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation, temperature, pain score. This is when a hematoma announces itself and when anesthesia nausea peaks. Your nurse gets ahead of both instead of reacting to them.

  3. HOURS 4–8 · SETTLING IN

    First walk, first sips, first drain check

    Your nurse gets you up for the first time with hands on you, so you don't faint on a bathroom floor. Drains stripped and measured. Fluids started slowly. Anti-nausea medication timed before the pain medication, not after it.

  4. OVERNIGHT · YOU SLEEP, YOUR NURSE DOESN'T

    Awake and watching

    Medications land on schedule, which means you wake up comfortable instead of waking up in pain and chasing it. Your nurse checks your breathing, your garment, your position and your drains all night. Family members go to bed.

  5. MORNING · HANDOVER

    A written record for your surgeon

    You get a full chart of the night: vitals, drain volumes, medication times, intake and output. Your surgeon's office gets a clinical picture instead of "she said she felt okay." Whoever takes over from your nurse gets taught what to watch for.

What we recover

Recovery care for the procedures Toronto surgeons do most

Every procedure has its own risks in the first 24 hours. Care is built around yours, not a generic checklist.

Tummy Tuck Recovery Nurse

Abdominoplasty often involves surgical drains, restricted positioning, and an increased risk of blood clots. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse monitors vital signs, measures and records JP or Hemovac drain output, assesses for bleeding, manages prescribed compression garments, and assists with safe mobility. Your nurse also recognizes early signs of postoperative complications requiring medical attention.

BBL Recovery Nurse

After a Brazilian butt lift, you can't sit or lie directly on your back during the initial recovery period, as directed by your surgeon. Pressure on the buttocks can compromise the transferred fat. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse maintains prescribed positioning, monitors breathing and circulation following anesthesia, assesses for bleeding, manages postoperative drainage, and assists with safe transfers while protecting the surgical areas.

360 Liposuction Recovery

Extensive circumferential liposuction can involve significant body fluid shifts, heavy postoperative drainage, and bleeding, potentially leading to changes in blood pressure. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse monitors vital signs, measures and records drainage, manages the compression garment, assesses circulation, and recognizes early signs of hypovolemia or other postoperative complications.

Mommy Makeover Recovery

Combining breast surgery, abdominoplasty, and liposuction can involve longer anesthesia, multiple surgical sites, drains, and increased postoperative pain. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse monitors respiratory and cardiovascular status, assesses surgical sites, measures and records drain output, manages prescribed compression garments, and evaluates pain and medication effects. Your nurse also monitors for bleeding and other postoperative complications.

Facelift and Neck Lift Recovery

Facial surgery requires careful blood pressure monitoring, appropriate head elevation, and close observation for bleeding or increasing swelling. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse assesses surgical dressings and drains, monitors vital signs, evaluates pain and medication effects, and watches for expanding hematomas. Following neck surgery, rapidly increasing swelling can compromise the airway and requires immediate medical attention.

Hotel Recovery for Out-of-Town Patients

Travelling to Toronto for surgery means recovering away from home, often without experienced medical support nearby. Your ICU-trained and post-operative care registered nurse provides overnight postoperative monitoring, manages prescribed compression garments and surgical drains, assesses peripheral IV saline locks when present, and administers prescribed medications within authorized nursing orders. Your nurse also monitors for complications and communicates concerning changes to your surgical team.

Questions

What patients ask before they book

Do I need a nurse after a tummy tuck or 360 lipo?

Most Toronto surgeons require that you're not alone for the first 24 hours after a tummy tuck, 360 liposuction, BBL or any procedure done under general anesthesia. The first night is when hematoma, breathing problems, low blood pressure, nausea and fainting are most likely. Your private recovery nurse monitors your vital signs, manages your drains and pain medication, and responds right away if something changes while your surgeon's office is closed.

Who will be my nurse?

Every Toronto ProRN nurse is an experienced ICU Registered Nurse (RN) who is also trained in post-operative recovery after plastic and cosmetic surgery. They're not just Registered Nurses. Each one brings intensive care experience plus hands-on recovery training for procedures like tummy tucks, BBLs, 360 lipo and facelifts. All of our nurses are licensed by the College of Nurses of Ontario and in good standing, and you can check any nurse's registration on the CNO Registry. We never send Registered Practical Nurses (RPNs, called LPNs in other provinces) or personal support workers (PSWs) in their place. Toronto ProRN was founded by JoAnne Gomez, RN, who has 27 years in critical care and 14 years in plastic surgery recovery.

Can a nurse stay overnight with me after plastic surgery in Toronto?

Yes. Your nurse provides continuous overnight care at your home, hotel or recovery suite anywhere in the GTA. They stay awake through the night monitoring vital signs, tracking drain output, timing medications, managing nausea and helping you get up safely the first time.

Who takes care of my drains after a tummy tuck?

Your nurse does. Stripping the tubing, emptying and measuring output, recording volumes for your surgeon, and watching for clots, blockage or early infection. Most patients find drains the most stressful part of recovery, and having a nurse handle them removes the guesswork. Before your nurse leaves, they teach whoever is staying with you how to do it.

Can I recover in a hotel after plastic surgery in Toronto?

Yes, and it's common for patients flying into Toronto for surgery. Your nurse provides discreet care in your hotel room, including overnight monitoring before you're cleared to fly home, and follows your surgeon's post-operative instructions exactly. What's the difference between an ICU recovery nurse, a practical nurse and a PSW?

A personal support worker (PSW) or caregiver can bring you water and help you to the bathroom, but can't assess you. Registered Practical Nurses (RPNs) are trained to care for patients whose condition is stable and predictable, and the first night after surgery isn't always predictable. An ICU-trained Registered Nurse is used to patients who can change quickly. That means taking a full set of vitals and knowing what they mean, recognizing an expanding hematoma, judging whether your breathing is safe after anesthesia, managing opioid dosing and timing, and deciding when your surgeon needs to be called at 2am.

Is a private recovery nurse covered by OHIP?

No. OHIP doesn't cover private duty nursing or cosmetic recovery care, so this is paid privately. Some extended health plans reimburse private duty nursing. Check your policy and we'll give you a receipt to submit.

How far in advance should I book?

Two to eight weeks before your surgery date is ideal, especially for Friday operating days and the busy spring and December periods. Ontario statutory holidays may not be available to book, or may be charged at time and a half. If your surgery is sooner than that, send the request anyway. Cancellations happen, and we'll see if we can accommodate you.

Will my nurse coordinate with my surgeon?

Yes. Your nurse follows your surgeon's written post-operative orders exactly and keeps a written record of vitals, drain output, medication times and intake. That record goes to the office or comes with you to your first follow-up.

Will I be getting a female nurse?

Yes. Many patients prefer a female nurse after a BBL or breast surgery. Tell us when you book if you'd prefer a male nurse. Otherwise, your nurse will be female. Privacy, modesty and discretion are part of every booking, and nothing about your surgery is discussed with anyone outside your care.
